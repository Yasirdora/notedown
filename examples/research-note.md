# Returning to a draft after an interruption

> Example document. Enable the Footnotes pack in Notedown's command palette
> to render the references below. With the pack off, they remain plain text.

## Question

Which parts of a writing workspace help a person return to a draft after a
brief interruption?

This fictional study plan uses a small observation exercise to explore the
question. It does not present measured findings.

## Method

Ask a participant to write a short explanation of a familiar activity.
Introduce a brief interruption, then observe how they resume. Repeat with
the same writing surface after hiding optional controls.[^order]

Record the first action on returning, the location of the caret, and whether
the participant rereads the previous paragraph before adding text.

## Observation sheet

| Observation | Session one | Session two |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First action after returning | To record | To record |
| Found the previous writing position | To record | To record |
| Reread before continuing | To record | To record |
| Participant's explanation | To record | To record |

## Interpretation

A shorter pause could suggest that the workspace makes the current task
easier to recognize. It could also reflect familiarity with the exercise.
Ask the participant what they noticed before drawing a conclusion.[^limits]

Separate direct observations from explanations. “Reread two sentences” is an
observation; “felt distracted” needs the participant's account.

## Next step

Use the first session to improve the observation sheet. Keep the question
narrow and avoid treating an informal trial as a general result.

[^order]: In a later round, vary the order of the two conditions so practice is less likely to explain the difference.
[^limits]: A small exploratory exercise helps refine questions. It cannot establish how every writer will respond.
